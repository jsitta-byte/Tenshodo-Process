# Commercialization Module

## Purpose

This optional module helps an organization turn a real capability, product, or service into a coherent market-facing offer without mixing client-specific commercial state into Tenshodo Process.

Activate the module when there is real durable work such as:

- a new service or product must be taken to market;
- an existing offer lacks coherent positioning or messaging;
- salespeople need repeatable collateral and enablement;
- brand identity needs to be established or refreshed;
- proof/claims need governance;
- marketing and revenue execution need a shared durable source.

Do not activate it merely because a target org chart contains CMO, CRO, CPO, or CFO roles.

## Commercialization spine

Use this sequence as a dependency-aware starting point:

**market evidence → offer architecture → brand/positioning → messaging/proof → sales enablement → marketing activation → learn/revise**

These activities can overlap when dependencies are explicit.

## Functional boundaries

### Product / service function

Answers: **What exactly are we selling?**

Typical responsibilities:

- offer scope;
- service/product architecture;
- package boundaries;
- lifecycle;
- roadmap;
- deliverable definition.

### Marketing function

Answers: **For whom, why does it matter, and how do we explain it?**

Typical responsibilities:

- brand;
- positioning;
- category language;
- messaging;
- content;
- external communications;
- visual identity direction;
- demand-generation strategy.

### Revenue / sales function

Answers: **How do we turn the offer into qualified pipeline and closed business?**

Typical responsibilities:

- sales motion;
- qualification;
- discovery;
- objections;
- pipeline;
- sales enablement;
- commercial execution.

### Finance function

Answers: **Does the commercial model make economic sense?**

Typical responsibilities:

- pricing economics;
- margin/unit economics;
- discounting guardrails;
- financial controls.

These are target accountability boundaries, not automatic instructions to create executive roles.

## Activation rule

Activate the smallest set of functions required by current work.

Examples:

- Brand identity and market narrative exist as real work → marketing/CMO perspective may be justified.
- A seller needs a repeatable sales motion → revenue/CRO perspective may be justified.
- The offer itself is ambiguous → product/CPO perspective may be required.
- Pricing or margin decisions become material → finance/CFO perspective may be required.

Use the organizational-bootstrap method when the human steward is unknown.

## Required commercial foundation

Before producing large volumes of collateral, establish durable source artifacts.

Minimum recommended set:

1. Brand & Market Foundation.
2. Offer Architecture.
3. Claims / Proof Register.
4. Sales Enablement Handoff.

Templates live in `templates/COMMERCIALIZATION_*`.

## Brand is more than visual identity

A commercialization-ready brand should cover:

- purpose;
- target customer / ICP;
- problem and stakes;
- category / frame of reference;
- positioning;
- differentiated value proposition;
- proof points;
- message hierarchy;
- voice and tone;
- visual direction;
- approved and unapproved claims;
- relationship between master brand and offers.

Logos, colors, typography, and design systems are downstream expressions of this foundation.

## Claims governance

Do not allow sales or marketing collateral to silently convert an attractive statement into an accepted fact.

Each material external claim should identify:

- claim text;
- claim type;
- supporting evidence;
- evidence owner/source;
- approval state;
- allowed contexts;
- expiration/revalidation need;
- known qualification or limitation.

See `templates/COMMERCIALIZATION_CLAIMS_REGISTER.md`.

## Asset generation rule

Sales decks, marketing decks, one-pagers, landing pages, case studies, campaigns, and scripts should be generated from the durable commercial foundation rather than independently reinventing the narrative.

Recommended dependency chain:

**foundation → offer → claims → message architecture → asset brief → asset → field feedback**

## Sales / marketing collaboration

Marketing may own the narrative architecture while revenue owns the selling motion.

A sales deck is therefore usually a shared artifact:

- marketing governs positioning/message consistency;
- product/service governs offer truth;
- revenue tests usefulness in selling;
- finance/legal/risk review where pricing, commitments, claims, or obligations require them.

Do not interpret collaboration as shared final authority for every decision.

## Working-plane / control-plane split

Client brand files, creative assets, decks, customer research, pricing, campaign state, and market-sensitive materials belong in the client's authorized working/control environment.

Tenshodo Process retains only sanitized methodology, schemas, templates, and validated lessons.

## Module outputs

A completed initial commercialization foundation should make it possible to answer:

- What are we selling?
- Who is it for?
- What problem does it solve?
- Why should the buyer believe us?
- How do we explain it consistently?
- Which claims are validated?
- How should a salesperson introduce and qualify it?
- What still requires human adoption or specialist review?

## Suggested initial workstream

Use a client workstream such as `COM — Commercialization / Brand` when commercialization has enough durable work to justify its own cursor.

Revenue, product, and finance may remain separate workstreams when their workloads become independently executable.
